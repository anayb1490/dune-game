1. OVERVIEW & THEME
================================================================================

Frank Herbert's classic science fiction novel Dune is the setting for this
game of conquest, diplomacy, and betrayal. Players take on the roles of the
major factions vying for control of the desert planet Arrakis — known
universally as Dune.

Dune is the only place in the galaxy where spice (melange) can be found.
Spice is the key to interstellar travel: the Spacing Guild's Steersmen can
only navigate hyperspace by ingesting it. Spice is also a geriatric medicine
that prolongs life. With spice, one can buy whatever one wants. The entire
economic structure of the Imperium is based upon it.

Desert sandscapes cover most of Dune's surface, broken only by giant rock
ridges. Giant sandworms (called Shai-Hulud by the Fremen) — up to a quarter
mile in length — live beneath the sand and attack anyone who lingers on it.
Terrifying coriolis storms can also ravage the surface.

All players are subject to the economics of the CHOAM merchant combine.
Resources are expensive, shipping is costly, excellence has a price — and
that price must be paid in spice.

The game is divided into a Basic Game and an Advanced Game. Players are
encouraged to learn and play the Basic Game several times before attempting
the Advanced Game.


================================================================================
2. THE SIX FACTIONS
================================================================================

ATREIDES
--------
Led by the youthful Paul Atreides (Muad'Dib) — rightful heir to the planet,
gifted with valiant lieutenants and a strange partial awareness of the future,
but beset by more powerful and treacherous opponents.

BENE GESSERIT
-------------
Represented by Reverend Mother Gaius Helen Mohiam — ancient and inscrutable,
carefully trained in psychological control and a genius at achieving her ends
through the efforts of others.

EMPEROR
-------
His majesty the Padishah Emperor Shaddam IV — keen and efficient, yet easily
lulled into complacency by his own trappings of power.

SPACING GUILD
-------------
Represented by steersman Edric (in league with smuggler bands) — monopolist
of transport, yet addicted to ever-increasing spice flows.

FREMEN
------
Represented by the planetary ecologist Liet-Kynes — commanding fierce hordes
of natives, adept at life and travel on the planet, and dedicated to preventing
any outside control while bringing about Dune's own natural regeneration.

HARKONNEN
---------
Led by the decadent Baron Vladimir Harkonnen — master of treachery and cruel
deeds.


================================================================================
3. GAME COMPONENTS
================================================================================

GAME BOARD
----------
The game board is a map of the planet Dune showing four types of territories:

  - Sand:        Yellow or tan with a single border.
  - Rock:        Brown with double borders.
  - Strongholds: Red-brown with triple borders.
  - Polar Sink:  Mottled white with a double border.

Some territories are marked with a Spice Blow icon to show the locations of
possible Spice Blows.

The map is divided by longitude lines into eighteen sectors, which extend from
the edge of the Polar Sink to the equator at the edge of the board. Sectors
control the way the storm moves around the board.

There are six player circles around the map to help determine play order. The
map also includes a Turn Track and the Tleilaxu Tanks for holding dead leaders
and forces awaiting revival.

CARDS
-----
  - Spice Deck (21 cards): Tells where Spice Blows will create treasure troves
    of spice, and when the giant sandworms (Shai-Hulud) will appear.
  - Treachery Deck (33 cards): Provides weapons, defenses, tricks, and tools
    to outmaneuver opponents and win battles.
  - Traitor Deck (30 cards): One card for each Leader Disc; these are leaders
    from other factions that a player has subverted.
  - Alliance Deck (6 cards): Reminds players of their Alliance benefits.
  - Storm Deck (16 cards): For use with Fremen advanced game advantages.
  - Bene Gesserit Prediction Deck (6 faction cards + 10 turn cards): Used to
    predict who will win the game and on which turn.

TOKENS
------
  - 156 Spice Tokens (48 each of 1 and 2, 36 of 5, 24 of 10)
  - Storm Marker
  - Game Turn Counter
  - Destroyed Shield Wall Token
  - Sandworm Token (advanced Fremen use)
  - Kwisatz Haderach Card and Counter tokens (advanced Atreides use)

SIX PLAYER FACTION SETS
------------------------
Each faction set contains:
  1. A Player Shield bearing the faction's main character and emblem.
  2. A single- or double-sided Player Sheet describing the faction's Basic and
     Advanced Game advantages.
  3. Player Markers (Atreides: green; Bene Gesserit: blue; Emperor: red;
     Fremen: sand; Spacing Guild: orange; Harkonnen: black).
  4. 5 large Leader Discs, each showing a leader and their fighting strength.
  5. 20 small tokens called forces. Starred forces have special significance
     only in the Advanced Game.

Special Force Notes:
  - Emperor: 5 starred forces (Sardaukar) are worth twice their normal value
    in battle against all opponents except Fremen.
  - Fremen: 3 starred forces (Fedaykin) are worth twice their normal value
    in battle and in taking losses.
  - Bene Gesserit: Forces have two sides — the striped "advisor" side and the
    plain "fighter" side.

BATTLE WHEELS
-------------
The game includes 2 Battle Wheels, which must be assembled and joined with
the center pin before play begins. These are used to secretly dial battle
commitments.


================================================================================
4. SETUP FOR PLAY (BASIC GAME)
================================================================================

Step 1: SPICE BANK
Place all spice tokens in the Spice Bank.

Step 2: DECKS
Shuffle the Spice Deck and Treachery Deck and place them face down next to the
game board. Played cards are piled face up next to the decks as discards and
reshuffled to restock decks as necessary.

Step 3: FACTION SELECTION
Players choose factions. Two methods:
  (a) Shuffle the faction cards from the Prediction Deck and deal 1 to each
      player at random, OR
  (b) Each player randomly draws from the Bene Gesserit turn prediction cards
      and selects a faction in order of drawn card (highest card chooses first).
Players may swap factions with each other at this point.

Step 4: POSITIONS
Each player places their Player Marker on the player circle closest to their
shield and seat at the table.

Step 5: TRAITORS
Remove the cards for factions not in play from the Traitor Deck. Shuffle
thoroughly. Deal 4 cards to each player.
  - Each player secretly selects 1 card to keep behind their shield.
  - If a player drew any leaders from an opponent's faction, they may choose 1
    to become their traitor.
  - If a player drew no opponent leaders, they may protect one of their own
    leaders.
  - The other 3 cards are returned face down to the bottom of the Traitor Deck.

Step 6: SPICE
Each player receives spice from the Spice Bank in the amount indicated on their
player sheet, placed behind their shield.

Step 7: FORCES
Each player places their forces on the board as indicated by their player sheet.
All reserves are placed next to their shield (off-planet).

Step 8: TREACHERY CARDS
Deal 1 Treachery Card from the Treachery Deck to each player.

Step 9: TURN MARKER
Place the Turn Marker at position 1 on the Turn Track.

IMPORTANT NOTE: A faction's special advantages always take precedence over
the general rules whenever they contradict them.

PLAYER COUNT VARIANTS
---------------------
  - 2 Players: Recommended Atreides vs. Harkonnen, or play 2 factions each.
    Number of strongholds needed to win is increased to 4.
  - 3 Players: Recommended Atreides, Harkonnen, and Fremen. An Alliance needs
    4 strongholds to win (same as standard Alliance rules).
  - 4 Players: Consider leaving out both Spacing Guild and Bene Gesserit.
  - 5 Players: Consider playing with no Bene Gesserit.
  - 6 Players: Standard game with all factions.


================================================================================
5. OBJECT OF THE GAME
================================================================================

Each faction has unique economic, military, strategic, or treacherous
advantages. The object of the game is to use these advantages to gain control
of Dune.

The winner is the FIRST PLAYER to occupy at least 3 strongholds with at least
one of their forces during the Mentat Pause Phase.

A player may win ALONE or in an ALLIANCE with other players.

The game runs a maximum of 10 turns. If no player has won after 10 turns,
special faction victory conditions may apply (see Spacing Guild and Fremen
faction sheets below).


================================================================================
6. SEQUENCE OF PLAY
================================================================================

DUNE is played in turns to a maximum of 10 turns. Each turn consists of nine
phases that must be completed in the exact sequence listed below.


------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 1: STORM
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Storm Marker is moved around the map. The faction whose Player Marker the
storm next approaches becomes the First Player for that turn.

FIRST STORM (Turn 1 only):
The Storm Marker is placed at a random starting location using the following
procedure:
  - The two players whose player circles are nearest on either side of the
    Storm Start Sector each secretly dial a number from 0 to 20 on the Battle
    Wheels.
  - Both numbers are simultaneously revealed, totaled, and the Storm Marker is
    moved from the Storm Start Sector counterclockwise around the map by that
    total number of sectors.

SUBSEQUENT STORM MOVEMENT:
  - The two players who last used the Battle Wheels each independently dial a
    number from 1 to 3.
  - Both numbers are simultaneously revealed, added together.
  - The Storm Marker advances counterclockwise from its current position by
    the total number of sectors.

STORM DAMAGE:
  - Any forces in a sector of SAND TERRITORY (except the Imperial Basin) over
    which the storm passes or stops are KILLED. These forces go to the Tleilaxu
    Tanks.
  - Forces NOT on a sand territory are protected from the storm.
  - Any spice in a sector over which the storm passes or stops is removed to
    the Spice Bank.

STORM OBSTRUCTION:
  - Forces may NOT move into, out of, or through a sector in storm.
  - Forces may NOT battle if either force is in storm.

FIRST PLAYER:
  - The player whose Player Marker the storm next approaches is the First Player
    in the Bidding Phase, Shipping Phase, and Movement Phase.


------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 2: SPICE BLOW AND NEXUS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Turn over the top card of the Spice Deck. There are two possible card types:

TERRITORY CARD (Spice Blow):
  - The amount of spice shown on the card is taken from the Spice Bank and
    placed onto the territory in the sector containing the Spice Blow icon.
  - The card is placed face up on the Spice Deck discard pile.
  - If the Spice Blow icon is currently in storm, no spice is placed that turn.

SHAI-HULUD CARD (Sandworm):
  - All spice and forces in the territory shown on the card currently face up
    on the discard pile are removed to the Spice Bank and Tleilaxu Tanks
    respectively.
  - The Shai-Hulud card is placed face up on the Spice Deck discard pile.
  - Another card is turned over. If it is also Shai-Hulud, it is immediately
    discarded and another card is turned over. This continues until a Territory
    Card appears. Spice is placed as normal and that Territory Card goes to the
    discard pile.
  - Revealing a Shai-Hulud card after the first turn causes a NEXUS at the end
    of the phase (see Alliances section).

FIRST TURN EXCEPTION:
  - During the first turn's Spice Blow Phase only, all sandworm cards turned
    over are ignored and set aside, then reshuffled back into the Spice Deck
    after this phase. No Nexus occurs on turn 1.


------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 3: CHOAM CHARITY
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Any player with 0 or 1 spice may call out "CHOAM Charity" and collect spice
to bring their total up to 2.


------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 4: BIDDING
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Players bid spice to acquire Treachery Cards from the Treachery Deck.

DECLARATION:
Before bidding starts, all players must declare how many Treachery Cards they
hold. The hand limit is 4 cards. Players with 4 cards must pass during all
bidding.

DEALING:
One player deals cards from the Treachery Deck face down in a row, one card
for each player who is allowed to bid (i.e., each player without a full hand).

AUCTION:
The first card in the row is auctioned off.
  - Bidding begins with the First Player (or the next eligible player to the
    right if the First Player has 4 cards).
  - The first bidder must bid 1 or more spice, or pass.
  - Bidding proceeds to the bidder's immediate right.
  - Each subsequent bidder may raise the bid or pass.
  - This continues until one player has the top bid and all others have passed.
  - The winning bidder pays the bid amount into the Spice Bank and takes the
    card.

BID LIMIT: Players may not bid more spice than they currently have.

ROTATING START:
For each subsequent card, the next eligible player to the right of the player
who opened the previous card's bidding begins the bidding. This ensures every
eligible player gets a chance to open bidding on a card.

END OF BIDDING:
Bidding ends when:
  (a) All cards have been auctioned off, OR
  (b) A card is passed on by everyone — in which case all remaining cards are
      returned to the top of the Treachery Deck and the phase ends.

TRANSPARENCY:
The NUMBER (not the type) of Treachery Cards each player holds must always be
open to everyone during the Bidding Phase. No one may hide the count of their
cards. A player with a full hand (4 cards) must pass on all bids.

TIME LIMIT:
Each player must bid within 10 seconds of the previous player or is assumed
to have passed.


------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 5: REVIVAL
------------------------------------------------------------------------

FORCE REVIVAL:
All players may revive up to 3 forces from the Tleilaxu Tanks.
  - FREE: A certain number of forces are revived for free (as stated on the
    player sheet).
  - BY SPICE: Additional forces beyond the free amount cost 2 spice per force,
    paid to the Spice Bank.
  - LIMIT: A player can never revive more than 3 forces per turn total
    (free + paid combined).
  - TO RESERVES: All revived forces must be placed in the player's off-planet
    reserve, not directly onto the board.

LEADER REVIVAL:
  - A player may revive a leader only if ALL 5 of their leaders are currently
    in the Tleilaxu Tanks.
  - To revive a leader, the player pays that leader's fighting strength in
    spice to the Spice Bank.
  - One leader may be revived per turn under this condition.
  - A revived leader can be played normally and is still subject to being a
    traitor.
  - If a revived leader is killed again, it is placed face down in the Tleilaxu
    Tanks and cannot be revived again until all of the player's other revivable
    leaders have been revived, killed, and returned to the Tanks.


------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 6: SHIPMENT AND MOVEMENT
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Starting with the First Player and proceeding COUNTERCLOCKWISE, each player
conducts their Force Shipment and then their Force Movement.

--- FORCE SHIPMENT ---

SHIPMENT OF RESERVES:
A player with off-planet reserves may make ONE shipment of any number of forces
from their reserves to any one territory on the map.

PAYMENT:
  - 1 spice per force shipped into any STRONGHOLD.
  - 2 spice per force shipped into any OTHER TERRITORY.
All shipment spice is paid to the Spice Bank (exception: paid to the Spacing
Guild when that faction is in play — see Spacing Guild faction sheet).

SECTORS:
When shipping into a territory lying in several sectors, the player must declare
in which sector they choose to leave their forces.

SHIPMENT RESTRICTIONS:
  - No player may ship into a sector in storm.
  - No player may ship into a stronghold already occupied by forces of two
    other factions.
  - No player may ship forces FROM the board BACK to their reserves (one-way
    only — exception: Spacing Guild).

--- FORCE MOVEMENT ---

BASIC MOVEMENT:
Each player may move, as a group, any number of their forces from one territory
into one other territory (one move total per turn).

Forces are free to move into, out of, or through any territory occupied by any
number of forces, subject to the restrictions below.

ORNITHOPTER MOVEMENT (3-territory move):
A player who begins their force move with one or more of their forces in either
Arrakeen, Carthag, or both has access to ornithopters and may move forces
through up to three adjacent territories.
  - The forces being moved do NOT have to originate in Arrakeen or Carthag.
  - Example: A player with forces in Arrakeen could move forces starting at
    Tuek's Sietch through Pasty Mesa and Shield Wall to the Imperial Basin.

STANDARD MOVEMENT (1-territory move):
A player without any force in either Arrakeen or Carthag at the start of their
move may only move forces to one adjacent territory.

ONE FORCE MOVE PER TURN:
Each player may make only one force move per turn.

SECTORS AND MOVEMENT:
Sectors have NO effect on movement. Forces can move into or through a territory
ignoring all sector divisions. A sector's only function is to regulate storm
movement/damage and spice collection.

STORM MOVEMENT RESTRICTIONS:
No force may move into, out of, or through a sector in storm.
  - Many territories span several sectors, so a player may move into and out
    of a territory partially in storm, so long as the group does not pass
    through the storm sector.
  - When ending a move in a territory lying in several sectors, the player must
    declare which sector their forces occupy.
  - The Polar Sink is NEVER in storm.

STRONGHOLD BLOCKING:
Forces cannot be moved into or through a stronghold if forces of two other
factions are already there.


------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 7: BATTLES
------------------------------------------------------------------------

BATTLE DETERMINATION:
Wherever two or more factions' forces occupy the same territory, battles must
occur. Battles continue until only one faction's forces (or no forces) remain
in each territory on the map.

EXCEPTIONS (no battle required):
  - Two factions' forces in the same territory separated by a sector in storm
    may not battle. Their forces may remain in the same territory.
  - Forces in the Polar Sink never battle. It is a free haven for all factions.

AGGRESSOR ORDER:
The First Player is named the aggressor first. The aggressor chooses the order
in which they wish to fight their battles. Then the player to their immediate
right becomes the aggressor, and so on, until all battles are resolved.

If three or more factions are in the same territory, the current aggressor picks
who they fight first, second, etc., for as long as they survive.

--- CREATING THE BATTLE PLAN ---

To resolve a battle, each player secretly formulates a Battle Plan using a
Battle Wheel. A Battle Plan always includes:
  1. A number dialed on the Battle Wheel (from 0 up to the total number of
     forces the player has in the disputed territory).
  2. A leader (or cheap hero card), if possible.
  3. Optionally, Treachery Cards (weapon, defense, or both).

BATTLE WHEEL:
Each player secretly dials a number from 0 to the number of forces they have
in the disputed territory. BOTH players will lose the number of forces they
dial, win or lose.

LEADERS:
  - Select one Leader Disc and place it face up in the slot on the wheel.
  - A Cheap Hero Card may be played in lieu of a Leader Disc.
  - A player MUST play either a leader or a cheap hero as part of their Battle
    Plan if possible.
  - If no leader or cheap hero is available, the player must announce that fact.
  - Leaders who survive a battle may fight more than once in a single territory
    if needed, but no leader may fight in more than one territory during the
    same phase.

NO LEADER SITUATION:
A player with no leader or cheap hero must still battle, but may NOT play any
Treachery Cards as part of their Battle Plan. (This can occur when all leaders
are in the Tleilaxu Tanks and the player has no cheap hero.)

CHEAP HERO:
When a player plays a cheap hero, their total is simply the number of forces
dialed, but they may still play weapon, defense, or worthless cards.

TREACHERY CARDS:
Players with a leader or cheap hero MAY play:
  - A Weapon Treachery Card
  - A Defense Treachery Card
  - Both
  - Neither
Cards are held against the wheel secretly until both players reveal simultaneously.

--- REVEALING AND RESOLVING ---

REVEALING:
When both players are ready, Battle Plans are revealed simultaneously.

WINNER DETERMINATION:
The winner is the player with the higher total of:
  (number dialed on the Battle Wheel) + (leader's fighting strength)

  - A leader killed by a weapon card does NOT count toward the total.

TIES:
In the case of a tie, the AGGRESSOR wins.

WEAPONS:
  - If a player's opponent played a Weapon Treachery Card and the player did
    NOT play the proper Defense Treachery Card against it, the player's leader
    is KILLED and does not count toward their total.
  - Both leaders can be killed simultaneously if both weapon/defense
    combinations match/fail.

KILLED LEADERS:
  - Any leaders killed are immediately placed face up in the Tleilaxu Tanks.
  - The WINNER immediately receives the killed leaders' fighting strength value
    (including their own killed leader if applicable) in spice from the Spice Bank.

SURVIVING LEADERS:
Leaders who survive remain in the territory until all battles in other
territories are resolved. Then their owners retrieve them.

LOSER'S CONSEQUENCES:
  - Loses ALL forces they had in the territory to the Tleilaxu Tanks.
  - Must discard every Treachery Card used in their Battle Plan.
  - Does NOT lose their leader as a result of losing (leaders are only killed
    by Weapon Treachery Cards).

WINNER'S CONSEQUENCES:
  - Loses only the number of forces they DIALED on the Battle Wheel (these go
    to the Tleilaxu Tanks).
  - May keep or discard any Treachery Cards they played.
  - Receives spice for any killed leaders (own or opponent's).

--- TRAITORS ---

If a player is in a battle and their opponent uses a leader that matches a
Traitor Card that player controls, they may call out "Treachery!" and pause
the game.

REVEALING THE TRAITOR:
The Traitor Card is revealed. Then:

The player who revealed the Traitor Card:
  - Immediately wins the battle.
  - Loses nothing, regardless of what was played in the Battle Plans (even if
    a lasgun and shield were revealed).
  - Places the traitorous leader in the Tleilaxu Tanks and receives that
    leader's fighting strength in spice from the Spice Bank.

The player whose traitor was revealed:
  - Loses all of their forces in the territory.
  - Discards all cards they played.

TWO TRAITORS:
If both leaders are traitors for the opposing player, BOTH players lose all
their forces in the territory, all cards played, and both leaders. Neither
player receives any spice.

OPTIONAL: A player MAY voluntarily choose not to reveal a traitor if it is
played against them in battle.


------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 8: SPICE COLLECTION
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Any player with forces in a sector of a territory containing spice may now
collect that spice.

COLLECTION RATES:
  - 3 spice per force if the player occupies Carthag or Arrakeen.
  - 2 spice per force if the player does NOT occupy Carthag or Arrakeen.

Spice tokens are taken from the territory and placed behind the player's shield.

Uncollected spice remains in place for future turns.


------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 9: MENTAT PAUSE
------------------------------------------------------------------------

WIN CHECK:
  - If a player occupies 3 strongholds with at least one of their forces,
    that player wins the game ALONE.
  - If two allied players together occupy a total of 4 strongholds (when the
    4-stronghold alliance victory condition applies) with at least one of their
    forces each, that Alliance wins the game.

NO WINNER:
If no one wins, players evaluate their positions, then advance the Turn Marker
to the next position on the Turn Track to begin the next turn.

If the Turn Marker reaches 10 and no one has won, the Spacing Guild Special
Victory Condition applies (see Spacing Guild sheet). If the Spacing Guild is
not in play, the Fremen Special Victory Condition applies (see Fremen sheet).
If neither condition is met, the player occupying the most strongholds wins.
If several players qualify equally, all of them win.


================================================================================
7. ALLIANCES (NEXUS)
================================================================================

A NEXUS is triggered when a Shai-Hulud (sandworm) card is turned over during
the Spice Blow and Nexus Phase on the SECOND TURN OR LATER. (Sandworm cards
on turn 1 are ignored and cause no Nexus.)

During a Nexus, all players have a chance to make, join, or break Alliances.
Once players have had a chance to do so, play continues.

FORMING AN ALLIANCE
-------------------
  - No more than TWO players may be in a single Alliance.
  - With an Alliance active, the win condition becomes 4 strongholds (instead
    of 3) held collectively.
  - Players may discuss the advantages and disadvantages of allying before
    committing.
  - Alliance membership must be PUBLIC — Alliances cannot be secret. Players
    swap Alliance Cards as a reminder.
  - Multiple Alliances can be formed during a single Nexus, but no player can
    be in more than one Alliance.
  - Once all players have had a chance to ally, no further Alliances can be
    made until the next Nexus.

BREAKING AN ALLIANCE
--------------------
  - Any player may break an Alliance during a Nexus by simply announcing it.
  - Players who break from an Alliance may immediately join or form a new
    Alliance during that same Nexus.

HOW AN ALLIANCE FUNCTIONS
--------------------------
WINNING: Allied players' forces are considered collectively for victory
purposes. If together they hold 4 strongholds (one or more forces each) during
the Mentat Pause Phase, that Alliance wins.

CONSTRAINT: Allies may NOT enter any territory (except the Polar Sink) where
one of their allies already has a force. Allies may NEVER battle one another.

ALLY SECRECY: Allies may discuss strategy and share information secretly at
any time.

BIDDING: During the Bidding Phase, allies may help pay for each other's
Treachery Cards, allowing a player to bid more spice than they actually have.

MOVEMENT: During the Movement Phase, allies may pay for each other's shipments.

SHARED ADVANTAGES: Allies may assist one another as specified on their
individual player sheets (faction-specific benefits vary).

IMPORTANT NOTE: If two allied factions occupy the same territory at the start
of the next turn (having allied during the previous turn), one must move out
during the Shipment and Movement Phase. If the first faction to move does not
move out, the second faction must move out — or lose those forces to the
Tleilaxu Tanks.


================================================================================
8. SECRECY AND BRIBERY RULES
================================================================================

SECRECY:
Players are never REQUIRED to keep their cards, spice holdings, or traitor
selection secret. They are never OBLIGATED to reveal this information either.

  - Spice holdings should be kept behind the Player Shield.
  - The number (not type) of Treachery Cards held must be open during Bidding
    but can be kept secret at all other times.

BRIBERY AND DEALS:
Players who are NOT members of the same Alliance may make verbal deals or
bribes between one another.
  - Once made, deals and bribes must be stated aloud and MUST be honored.
  - A player cannot renege on a deal or bribe.
  - Spice may be part of a deal or bribe.

DEAL RESTRICTIONS:
A deal or bribe CANNOT involve the transfer or gift of:
  - Treachery Cards
  - Leaders
  - Forces
  - Faction advantages

This leaves secret information, future actions, and spice as valid deal
components.

A player cannot make a deal that would contravene the rules or their own
faction powers. These are the only limitations.


================================================================================
9. ADVANCED GAME RULES
================================================================================

The Basic Game is changed by:
  - Increasing the number of Spice Blows (Double Spice Blow)
  - Adding a spice income advantage for holding city strongholds and Tuek's
    Sietch (Increased Spice Flow)
  - Enhanced Karama Card powers (Advanced Karama Cards)
  - An advanced combat system with spice-supported forces (Advanced Combat)


------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED COMBAT
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Spice now plays a direct role in combat:

  - Each force used in battle is valued at its FULL strength if 1 spice is
    expended to support it.
  - A force NOT supported by 1 spice is valued at HALF strength.

When creating a Battle Plan, a player must add the amount of spice they plan to
expend to their Battle Wheel along with their force count.

  - If a traitor is revealed, the winner does NOT have to expend any spice.
  - Otherwise, spice used in the Battle Plan must go to the Spice Bank,
    win or lose.

HALF-STRENGTH INCREMENTS:
Half-strength can be indicated on the Battle Wheel by aligning the line between
numbers with the line under the window.

TAKING LOSSES:
The winner may take their losses in any manner as long as the total agrees with
the strength dialed and the spice expended.

Example: The Emperor has 1 Sardaukar (worth 2 forces) and 5 ordinary forces.
The Emperor dials a strength of 3 and expends 1 spice. When taking losses, the
Emperor may choose:
  - Lose 1 Sardaukar at full strength (2) + 2 ordinary forces at half strength
    (0.5 + 0.5 = 1), total = 3.
  - Lose 1 ordinary force at full strength (1) + 4 ordinary forces at half
    strength (4 x 0.5 = 2), total = 3.

FREMEN EXCEPTION: Fremen forces do NOT require spice to count at full strength.


------------------------------------------------------------------------
DOUBLE SPICE BLOW (Advanced Phase 2)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

In the Advanced Game, Phase 2 reveals TWO Spice Cards instead of one, using
two separate discard piles (A and B).

PROCEDURE:
Reveal the first Spice Card:
  - If TERRITORY: Place spice normally, put card on discard pile A.
  - If SHAI-HULUD: Process the sandworm as in the Basic Game (using discard
    pile A), then continue turning cards until a Territory Card appears. A Nexus
    occurs.

Then reveal the second Spice Card and repeat the same procedure using discard
pile B.


------------------------------------------------------------------------
INCREASED SPICE FLOW (Advanced Collection)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

During every Spice Collection Phase (Phase 8), city and smuggler stronghold
holders collect spice automatically:
  - Each occupant of CARTHAG collects 2 spice.
  - Each occupant of ARRAKEEN collects 2 spice.
  - The occupant of TUEK'S SIETCH collects 1 spice.

To qualify, a player needs only occupy the stronghold at the time of collection.
If a player occupies two of these strongholds, they collect for each.


------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVANCED KARAMA CARDS
------------------------------------------------------------------------

In the Advanced Game, when playing a Karama Card a player may EITHER:
  (a) Prevent one opponent from using one of their faction's advantages (as in
      the Basic Game), OR
  (b) Use their faction's unique one-time Karama special power (listed below).

After play, the Karama Card is discarded.

FACTION KARAMA POWERS:
  - Atreides: Look at any one player's entire Battle Plan.
  - Emperor: Revive up to three forces or one leader for free.
  - Fremen: Place the sandworm token in any sand territory. It is treated as a
    normal sandworm (does NOT signal a Nexus).
  - Spacing Guild: Stop one off-planet shipment of any one player.
  - Harkonnen: Take without looking any number of cards (up to the entire hand)
    from any one player. For each card taken, give that player one of your own
    cards in return.
  - Bene Gesserit: No unique Karama power listed (they may use any worthless
    card as a Karama Card).

NOTE: A Karama Card can be used to stop a player from using an advanced
advantage. The Fremen Karama sandworm does NOT signal a Nexus; only a
Shai-Hulud card from the Spice Deck does.

FREMEN

You are native to Dune and know its ways.

STARTING POSITION:
  - 10 forces distributed as you like among: Sietch Tabr, False Wall South,

  - 10 forces in reserves (on the far side of Dune / off-planet).
  - Start with 3 spice.
  - Free Revival: 3 forces per turn. You CANNOT buy additional revivals.

BASIC ADVANTAGES:

SHIPMENT: During shipment, you may bring any or all of your reserves for FREE
onto the Great Flat, or onto any one territory within two territories of the
Great Flat (subject to storm and occupancy rules). Do NOT count the Great Flat

SHAI-HULUD PROTECTION: If Shai-Hulud appears in a territory where you have

ride the sandworm and move some or all of the forces in that territory to any
territory (subject to storm and occupancy rules). Forces in the destination

FREMEN SPECIAL VICTORY CONDITION: If no faction has won by the end of the

Sietch, AND neither Harkonnen, Atreides, nor Emperor occupies Tuek's Sietch —

ALLIANCE BENEFIT: You may choose to protect (or not protect) your allies from
being devoured by sandworms. At your discretion, you may allow allied forces
to revive 3 forces for free during Revival. Your allies also win with you if you
win via the Fremen Special Victory Condition.

ADVANCED GAME ADVANTAGES:

STORM RULE: On the first turn, move the Storm Marker normally using Battle
Wheels. On all subsequent turns, you control storm movement using Storm Cards:
  - Randomly select a card from the Storm Deck, secretly look at it, and place

  - In the next Storm Phase, reveal that card's number. Move the storm
    counterclockwise that many sectors. Return the card to the Storm Deck,
    shuffle, and draw another card for the following turn.

SANDWORMS: During a Spice Blow, all additional sandworms that appear AFTER the
first can be placed by you in any sand territory you wish. Any forces there
(except yours) are devoured.

STORM LOSSES: If your forces are caught in a storm, only HALF are killed
(fractions rounded up). You may also bring reserves into a storm at half loss.

FEDAYKIN: Your three starred forces (Fedaykin) are worth TWO normal forces in
battle and in taking losses. They are each treated as ONE force for revival
purposes. Only one Fedaykin can be revived per turn.

BATTLES (Advanced): Your forces do NOT require spice to count at full strength.

HARKONNEN
------------------------------------------------------------------------

You excel in treachery.

STARTING POSITION:
  - 10 forces in Carthag.
  - 10 forces in reserve (off-planet).
  - Start with 10 spice.

BASIC ADVANTAGES:

TRAITORS: At the start of the game, when you draw 4 Traitor Cards, you KEEP

TREACHERY CARDS: You may hold up to 8 Treachery Cards (not 4). When you have

  - At the start of the game, you are dealt 2 Treachery Cards instead of 1.

  - Traitor Cards you hold may be used against your ally's opponents (if in

ALLIANCE BENEFIT: Traitor Cards you hold may be used against your ally's
opponent.

ADVANCED GAME ADVANTAGE:

CAPTURED LEADERS: Every time you WIN a battle, you may either:
  (a) Randomly select 1 leader from the loser (including the leader used in
      the battle if not killed, but excluding leaders already used elsewhere
      that turn) and place that Leader Disc face down into the Tleilaxu Tanks
      to gain 2 spice from the Spice Bank, OR
  (b) Keep the leader and use it once in a battle. After use, if it was not
      killed, you must return it to its faction.

When all of your own leaders have been killed, you must return all captured
leaders to their factions immediately. Killed captured leaders go to the
Tleilaxu Tanks (from which the leader's owning faction can revive them, subject

ATREIDES

You have limited prescience.

MOVEMENT: At the start of the Movement Phase, before anyone moves, you may

BATTLE: During the Battle Phase, you may force your opponent to reveal your

ALLIANCE BENEFIT: You may assist your allies by forcing their opponent to

ADVANCED GAME ADVANTAGE:

KWISATZ HADERACH: The Kwisatz Haderach card starts INACTIVE and may not be

  - Alive or dead, the Kwisatz Haderach has no effect on the rule governing

11. STRATEGY TIPS
================================================================================

GENERAL STRATEGY
----------------

Players in DUNE must:
  1. Amass spice.
  2. Convert it to effective weaponry, defenses, and strategic aids.
  3. Position forces to suddenly move into key strongholds and defend them.

The game is won by daring, strategy, mobility, and treachery.

THE STORM: Determines who is First Player. Being First Player is an advantage
in battles (ties go to First Player). Being LAST is an advantage in movement
(surprise takeovers by the last player cannot be countered that turn).

THE SPICE BLOW: Its location determines who gets new spice. Sandworms are
vital because they are the only way alliances can form during the diplomatic
Nexus.

BIDDING: Determines who gains fighting strength. Wise players keep several
cards to keep opponents guessing, while retaining enough spice to ship more
forces.

MOVEMENT: Determines battles, spice collection, and often the win. Stay within
striking distance of several strongholds. The Polar Sink is ideal for mobile
forces — it connects to most territories by short routes and is unaffected by
sandworms or storms. A player who moves last may go for the win if earlier
players left strongholds unprotected.

BATTLES: The key test of strength. With good weapons and defenses, a player can
swiftly reverse the balance. Watch what weapons and defenses have been played
and retained. Remember which leaders are known traitors. The loser must discard
their cards; the winner may keep theirs and collect spice for killed leaders.

WORTHLESS CARDS: Worthless cards can only be discarded by playing them in
battle. Players often sacrifice a single force against a large force just to
discard worthless cards, which can confuse opponents into wasting their own
forces on unnecessary defense.

ALLIANCES: Usually force an end to the game within a few turns. In larger
games it is difficult to win alone. Ally with someone who complements your
advantages — if you are strong strategically but short on resources, seek a
spice-rich ally; if you are rich, look for strategy or treachery in a partner.

FACTION-SPECIFIC STRATEGY TIPS
-------------------------------

FREMEN:
Your major handicap is poverty. You usually cannot purchase Treachery Cards for
several early turns because others outbid you. Be patient. Move forces into
vacant strongholds, avoiding battles until prepared. When you do battle, dial
high and sacrifice forces freely — your revival rate is high and revival is free.
Your game plan: bide your time, wait for accessible Spice Blows no one else
wants, build resources, then strike.

SPACING GUILD:
Your major handicap is your weak leaders and slow revival. You are vulnerable
early and should make stronger moves after building resources. Your major
advantage is cheap and flexible shipment — use this for surprise moves,
especially when moving last. If the game is out of reach, consider suicide
battles against strong players to weaken them and prevent a win until round 10,
which you automatically win.

BENE GESSERIT:
Your major handicap is your low revival rate — do not let large numbers of
forces go to the Tleilaxu Tanks. Your strength is the ability to win by
correctly predicting another player's win turn, secretly working for that
outcome. Voice makes you effective in battles. Bide your time while subtly
influencing the game.

ATREIDES:
Your handicap is that you must both purchase cards AND ship onto Dune, with no
income source other than surface spice — keeping you in constant battles. You
start with Arrakeen and thus ornithopter access from turn 1; protect this.
Your prescience gives you slight advantages in Spice Blow anticipation and in
Battle Plans.

EMPEROR:
Your handicap is that all forces start off-planet and must be shipped in,
often requiring a battle before you are ready. Your income from bidding means
you will rarely need spice badly — the bidding phase keeps you supplied. Expect
to concentrate on city strongholds for the movement advantage they provide.

HARKONNEN:
Your handicap is obtaining spice. You are at your relative greatest strength at
the start. Capitalize by quickly buying as many Treachery Cards as possible,
then surge into battle. Since you get 2 cards for every 1 you bid for, you can
afford to bid slightly higher — but do not spend so lavishly that you cannot
ship forces or buy cards later. Use your large card hand to flush out the
traitors you control.

12. QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS (OFFICIAL RULINGS)
================================================================================

TREACHERY CARDS

Q: Does the Family Atomics card have an immediate effect if blown when the
   Storm Marker is over Arrakeen, Imperial Basin, or Carthag?
A: The sector the storm currently occupies is never affected. All appropriate
   sectors into which the storm subsequently moves will be affected.

Q: Can the Family Atomics card be used if the Shield Wall or the adjacent
   territory is in storm?
A: Yes, in both cases.

Q: Does a lasgun/shield explosion destroy spice in the territory?
A: Yes. All forces in the territory are also lost, including those of players
   not involved in the battle.

Q: If a Karama Card prevents the Atreides from looking at Treachery Cards, does
   it affect just one card or all cards up for bid?
A: All cards up for bid that turn.

Q: Can a Tleilaxu Ghola card allow a leader to fight in the same phase they
   were killed?
A: Yes. A leader revived by this card during a Battle Phase can be used in that
   same Battle Phase.

Q: Is the Tleilaxu Ghola 5-force revival free?
A: Yes.

Q: (Advanced) Can a Karama Card be used to stop a player from using an advanced
   advantage?
A: Yes.

Q: (Advanced) Does the Karama sandworm called by the Fremen signal a Nexus?
A: No. A Nexus is signaled only by a Shai-Hulud card drawn from the Spice Deck.

REVIVAL

Q: Are revived leaders still subject to turning traitor?
A: Yes. Once a leader is a traitor, they are always a traitor for the entire
   game, even if revived.

Q: (Advanced) Can players revive leaders if other leaders have been captured
   by the Harkonnens?
A: Yes. A player can begin reviving their own leaders if they have no leaders
   available to play in battle (this includes Harkonnen too).

MOVEMENT

Q: When the Fremen bring reinforcements, must they include the Great Flat when
   counting the two-territory range?
A: No. Never include the Great Flat itself when counting the two-territory range.

Q: Is shipping into Arrakeen or Carthag and then using ornithopters allowed?
A: Yes. The designers have always allowed this. A fighting force can be shipped
   into Carthag or Arrakeen, and then use ornithopters to move other forces
   through up to three territories anywhere on the board.

Q: Can forces that moved into different sectors of the same territory at
   different times move or ship as a single group?
A: Yes.

Q: Does a sandworm move (Fremen riding Shai-Hulud) count as the Fremen's one
   group move per turn?
A: No. The sandworm move is made in the Spice Blow Phase after the Nexus, not
   in the Movement Phase.

Q: Do sectors affect movement?
A: No. Sectors function only to regulate storm movement/effect and spice
   placement/collection. They do not restrict movement between territories.

Q: If a storm is over Arrakeen or Carthag, does it affect the three-territory
   (ornithopter) move?
A: No, except of course that no forces may move into, out of, or through the
   storm itself.

Q: If the storm separates two factions in Pasty Mesa and on the next turn they
   can ally (Nexus occurs), and they do ally — how do they resolve the shared
   territory problem?
A: Yes, they can ally. If the First Player who ships and moves does not move
   forces out of that jointly occupied territory, the second player must do so —
   or lose those forces to the Tleilaxu Tanks.

BATTLE

Q: Can a player substitute a cheap hero for a weapon or defense card?
A: No. A cheap hero may only be played as a substitute for a leader.

Q: May the same weapon or defense card be played in more than one battle, if
   victorious?
A: Yes, including more than one battle in the same phase.

Q: Must forces that moved into different sectors of the same territory fight
   as a single group?
A: Yes (if storm permits).

Q: What happens if Bene Gesserit Voice, Atreides Prescience, and Truthtrance
   are all used in the same battle?
A: Bene Gesserit Voice must be used BEFORE Atreides Prescience. Truthtrance
   can be played at any time in this interaction. A player retains the ability
   to alter elements of their Battle Plan that were not affected by Voice,
   Prescience, or Truthtrance.

Q: Can a player voluntarily refuse to reveal a traitor played against them?
A: Yes. If your traitor appears in battle against you, you need not reveal it.

MISCELLANEOUS

Q: Do allies pay the Spacing Guild for all of their shipments?
A: Yes.

Q: What is the exact rate for Spacing Guild shipment back to reserves?
A: 1 spice per 2 forces (fractional costs rounded up).

Q: What happens if at the end of the last turn, no one has won and the Spacing
   Guild is not in play?
A: The Fremen win. If Fremen are also not in play, the player occupying the
   most strongholds wins. If several players qualify equally, all of them win.

Q: (Advanced) When reshuffling the Spice Deck, must you reshuffle the entire
   deck?
A: Yes.

Q: (Advanced) Does the Fremen get the sandworm advantage if a second sandworm
   appears in a Spice Phase?
A: Yes.

Q: How does the Spacing Guild pay for an odd number of forces shipped to
   reserves?
A: All fractional costs are rounded UP. A 5-force shipment costs 3 spice; a
   1-force shipment costs 1 spice; etc.

13. OPTIONAL RULES FOR ALLIANCES
================================================================================

The following optional rules modify how Alliances work in terms of player
count limits and stronghold win conditions. These are presented as variants
to achieve different game lengths and dynamics.

DEFAULT ALLIANCE GAME (Standard Rules):
  - Max 2 players per Alliance.
  - 4 strongholds needed to win (Allied).
  - Result: A longer game. Players seek synergy with one partner and must
    hold on to acquired strongholds together.

VARIANT — 2-PLAYER ALLIANCE LIMIT, 3 STRONGHOLDS TO WIN:
  - Max 2 players per Alliance.
  - 3 strongholds needed to win (Allied).
  - Result: A shorter game. Players look for synergy with one partner but
    target 3 strongholds rather than 4.

VARIANT — 3-PLAYER ALLIANCE LIMIT, 4 STRONGHOLDS TO WIN:
  - Up to 3 players may be in an Alliance.
  - 4 strongholds needed to win (Allied).
  - Result: A longer game. May devolve into 3 strongest vs. 3 weakest,
    but weaker players may reverse stronger players' fortunes.

VARIANT — 3-PLAYER ALLIANCE LIMIT, 3 STRONGHOLDS TO WIN:
  - Up to 3 players may be in an Alliance.
  - 3 strongholds needed to win (Allied).
  - Result: Probably the shortest game. May devolve into 3 strongest vs.
    3 weakest, but weaker players may pull off a surprise win.

VARIANT — ANY NUMBER OF PLAYERS IN ALLIANCES, 3 STRONGHOLDS TO WIN:
  - Any number of players may be in an Alliance.
  - 3 strongholds needed to win (Allied).
  - Result: A more exotic game. This is the original Dune rules Alliance
    game. Adversity creates ingenuity.
